# Business case runbook

## From first draft to the committee

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 4–6 weeks before | Agree the decision, the options (always with doing nothing) and the horizon; write the template | Sponsor, IT, finance |
| 3–4 weeks before | Fill the costs with their basis (quotes where possible) and the benefits with an owner and a KPI | IT programme manager, benefit owners |
| 2–3 weeks before | First build; fix every red finding; ask the owners of the main assumptions to validate them | Finance business partner |
| 1–2 weeks before | Second build; agree the recommendation and how to handle the downside; complete the paper | Sponsor, finance |
| The committee | Present the decision, the recommendation and its range, and the decisions needed | Sponsor |
| After | Record the outcome in the paper; keep the benefits register; review at 6 and 12 months | Benefit owners, PMO |

## Reading the result

1. **Reds first.** An option with a red finding on its costs or benefits is not ready. Correct the data (remove the double count, move the benefit to the right year, name the owner, add the missing line) rather than arguing the finding away.
2. **Then the assumptions.** An unvalidated assumption behind a large share of the benefits is the most common reason a business case fails after approval. A written quote, a signed contract or a measurement turns it green.
3. **Then the range.** Look at the pessimistic NPV and at the scenario that hurts most. If the option loses money in the pessimistic case, say so and propose how to limit it: phasing with a gate, a fixed-price contract, firmer quotes before signing.
4. **Last, the non-financial criteria.** They break ties and explain the recommendation; they do not rescue an option that does not pay.

## Presenting it

- Open with the decision and the recommendation in one sentence, with NPV, payback and the pessimistic case.
- Show the options as submitted and after the checks: the committee trusts a case that shows what was removed.
- Bring the decisions as choices with a proposal, an owner and a date.
- Keep soft benefits visible but outside the NPV.

## After the decision

- Write the outcome and the conditions in the paper's notes.
- Give each benefit owner their line of `benefits-register.csv` and put the 6 and 12 month reviews in the calendar.
- Re-run the case at each gate (end of a phase, before a major contract) with the actual costs; a gate that no longer passes is a decision for the committee, not for the project.
